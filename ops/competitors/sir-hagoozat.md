## 2. What the Egyptian market actually looks like

Read from the pages opened on 2026-11-12, not from an aggregator.

1. **The player app is expected to be free.** Yalla Hagz states the app is free on iOS and Android. No Egyptian page we opened shows a player price list. The money in this market is taken from the venue side (Yalla Hagz: free setup for venues plus a commission on the bookings brought to them, their wording).
2. **The owner tool is where the product is sold.** Kourtiva sells a system to pitch operators with a demo request. Playtomic sells four tiers to club managers. MATCHi and Pitchbooking sell venue software. Our owner plan must be self-serve and priced in EGP, because our buyer is a pitch owner in Cairo, not a club chain in Madrid.
3. **The deposit is the established pattern.** Yalla Hagz takes a small online deposit and the rest at the venue. Hagz Kora takes an InstaPay deposit. Nobody in the Egyptian set shows a full online payment of the whole slot. That is the gap for split payment and instant settlement.
4. **Squads are the real pain, and the best product already solved it.** Kourtiva's page names the four problems every pitch operator faces: the match is cancelled when players are missing, one person pays for the whole pitch, the evening is sold while the afternoon is empty, and a pitch is booked while the team never shows. Every one of those is a feature in our plan (open-match filling, split payment, off-peak pricing, QR entry with a scan log).
5. **Arabic is not done well.** Kourtiva's Arabic is formal and translated, and the two biggest consumer apps write their public pages in English. Egyptian dialect copy is free differentiation that no competitor is taking.
6. **Offline is not claimed by anyone.** No Egyptian competitor advertises working without a connection. On a phone at a pitch with patchy data, an offline pass and an offline schedule are a real advantage.
7. **Nobody sells owner analytics with numbers the owner cares about.** The owner pages we opened are booking calendars. Utilisation, no-show rate, cash on the night and repeat players are the numbers a pitch owner actually asks for.

## 3. Our edge, stated as five promises

1. **Egyptian Arabic first, English second.** Every screen written in Egyptian speech for players, MSA only in legal pages, English for the international market and for the owner console.
2. **One booking covers the whole squad.** The organiser books once, each player pays their own share when they join, and the slot is held until the group is funded or the hold expires.
3. **An empty slot finds a team.** Open matches show the missing places and push to nearby players by distance, level and position.
4. **Offline-first for players and owners.** The pass, the schedule and the cash register work with no connection and sync when it returns.
5. **Owners see the numbers that decide the business.** Utilisation by hour, no-show rate, cash collected, repeat players, and the cost of each channel, all on one owner page behind a server-verified owner token.

## 4. Market facts found in the second research pass (2026-11-12)

These are vendor-published numbers read today; they are planning inputs, not quotes we can promise.

**Payment gateway fees (Egypt).** Paymob publishes "2.75% + 3 EGP per transaction", no monthly or setup fee on the standard plan, weekly settlement (https://paymob.com/en/pricing, opened 2026-11-12). Kashier publishes the same 2.75% + 3 EGP on cards and lists wallets, Meeza, Basata kiosk and Apple Pay with wallet and kiosk rates quoted by sales (third-party comparison read 2026-11-12, "(unsourced, verify)" for the non-card rates). PayTabs publishes 2.5% + 3 EGP up to EGP 50,000/month volume and 2.5% + 2 EGP between EGP 30,001 and 250,000 (same source). Fawry kiosk/reference-number collection is contract-quoted, commonly cited at roughly 1.5% to 3.5% depending on method ("(unsourced, verify)"). VAT of 14% applies on the gateway fee itself in Egypt. Implication for our pricing: on a 300 EGP deposit the gateway keeps roughly 11 EGP before VAT on the fee, so a Venue Pro plan cannot absorb payment costs on small tickets; the fee schedule must be passed through or priced into the plan, and the plan must show the math before launch.

**Maps.** Google Maps Platform changed pricing on 2025-03-01: instead of a $200 monthly credit there are free monthly calls per SKU, 10,000 free calls per month for Essentials SKUs such as Dynamic Maps ($7.00 per 1,000 after that), and the Maps SDK and Embed are unlimited free (https://mapsplatform.google.com/pricing/ and the pricing FAQ, read 2026-11-12). Implication: the map view is effectively free at launch scale, but the API key still needs a billing account with a hard budget cap so a leaked key cannot run up charges.

**WhatsApp notifications.** Meta charges per delivered message on the WhatsApp Business Platform: for Egypt (+20) the October 2026 rate card lists utility and authentication templates at US$0.0036 each and marketing at US$0.0644; service replies inside the 24-hour window are charged after the first 1,000 per business number per month, and utility templates are charged even inside an open window (https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing and a rate summary read 2026-11-12). Implication: WhatsApp is cheap enough for booking confirmations and reminders as opt-in utility templates (about 0.11 EGP per message at the plan's exchange rate, "(unsourced, verify)"), but push and email stay the default channel and WhatsApp is an opt-in upgrade, never the only channel.

**Regulation.** Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020 Executive Regulations took effect 2025-11-01 with a one-year grace period ending 2026-10-31. Controllers must obtain licences from the new Personal Data Protection Centre, including separate licences for cross-border transfers and electronic marketing, and the law treats financial data as sensitive personal data with strict breach-notification duties and criminal penalties for unlicensed processing (Kluwer Global Privacy Law Review and legal500 country guide, read 2026-11-12). Implication: before any real user data is collected in Egypt, the legal pack must cover PDPL licensing, a data-protection officer appointment, the cross-border transfer position for Firestore (data stored outside Egypt), consent records for marketing messages, and breach notification runbooks. This is now a launch blocker alongside the lawyer review, not a later task.

**Category trends (2026).** Venue software is converging on: demand- and weather-aware dynamic pricing, waitlists that auto-promote when a slot frees, hybrid membership plus pay-and-play billing, venue-facing AI assistants that answer from the venue's own data (Thril ships an MCP connection and natural-language management), and no-show reduction via layered reminders (published vendor figures claim up to 48% fewer no-shows). Our plan already covers most of this; the waitlist auto-promotion and weather-aware voucher are added as features, and the owner AI assistant is scoped to read-only answers plus draft actions, never autonomous money movement.
