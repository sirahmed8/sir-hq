## 2. What the Egyptian and Arab market actually looks like

Read from the names and categories above, not from an aggregator.

1. **There is almost no Egyptian coverage anywhere.** Wheelmap's own country table does not list Egypt; the Arab entries it lists are Algeria, UAE and Turkey. The only Egyptian products are a dormant nonprofit database (~200 places), a 2018 iOS app, and a student project. That is the gap.
2. **The local survivors are nonprofits or students.** Entaleq (HELM) and أماكني both prove the idea resonates in Egypt, and both prove a solo nonprofit or a student team cannot keep a map product alive: stale data, no release cadence, no moderation machinery. A maintained product with a real release cadence is itself the edge.
3. **Nobody in the region does photo evidence plus second-user confirmation.** Every competitor either trusts one rating (Wheelmap's traffic light) or trusts an organisation (AccessNow). A place that publishes only after a photo and a confirmation is a trust model nobody local has.
4. **The owner-claim flow is the missing half.** أماكني let owners list places and then died; nobody running today lets a clinic, restaurant or mosque owner claim their entry, attach proof and keep it correct. That is a durable moat because businesses want correct information.
5. **Route planning that avoids barriers does not exist locally.** Wheelmap and Wheelmate find places; WheeLog logs routes users already took. Nobody offers "plan me a route and tell me which barrier it avoided" for an Egyptian city, where dropped curbs, unmanned lifts and blocked ramps change weekly.
6. **Accessibility information expires.** A lift breaks, a ramp is blocked by a cart, a toilet is closed for cleaning. Competitors show a rating with no date and no expiry. Dating every feature claim and re-verifying stale ones is a real product difference.
7. **Arabic is badly served.** Wheelmap supports Arabic among 32 languages but its data and UX are Europe-first. Entaleq is Arabic-first but dormant. Egyptian Arabic copy, RTL-first layout and bidi input are free differentiation.
8. **The audience is real and motivated.** Egypt has a large disability community, an ageing population, parents with strollers and tourists, all of whom need the same features (ramps, lifts, accessible toilets, parking, quiet spaces). The product serves all of them, which widens the base beyond wheelchair users alone.

## 3. Our edge, stated as five promises

1. **Only what is proven gets published.** A place entry goes live only with a photo, a feature list and a confirmation from a second user or a claim by the place owner. No untested single report becomes fact.
2. **The owner of the place can fix it.** A clinic, restaurant or mosque owner claims their entry, uploads proof and keeps it correct. Businesses want this; no local competitor offers it.
3. **Every claim has a date.** Accessibility features expire (a lift out of service, a blocked ramp). Each feature carries a verified-on date and a re-check window; stale data is flagged honestly instead of silently trusted.
4. **A route that tells you why.** Route planning avoids known barriers and explains each one it avoided ("lift at station B is reported out of service since 3 Nov"), instead of showing a green pin and hoping.
5. **Egypt and the Arab world first, in Egyptian Arabic.** Built for Cairo, Alexandria, Mansoura and the region, RTL-first, with the accessibility features that matter locally: dropped curbs, mosque entrances, clinic access, market aisles.
