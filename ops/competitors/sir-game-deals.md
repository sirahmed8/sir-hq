# Competitor study: SIR Game Deals (game discounts, free games and price history for Egypt)

Category: game deal aggregators, price history trackers, free game and giveaway trackers, console and PC key shops, Egyptian game retailers. Egypt and the Arab market first, then global.

Research date: 2026-11-12. Method: direct page fetches on 2026-11-12 for the pages that answered, plus a DuckDuckGo HTML index search on 2026-11-12 for the pages that blocked automation. Three probes were run against public endpoints with curl on 2026-11-12 (Steam appdetails with `cc=EG`, `cc=SA`, `cc=AE`, `cc=US`, `cc=TR`, the Steam Web API app list, the Epic free-games endpoint, the CheapShark API path and the FreeToGame API). Every number below is what the vendor's own page or our own probe returned today. Nothing was tested as a paying user, and no account was created on any site.

Truth rules for this file:

1. Every number that belongs to a competitor is their own claim or a listed price, written as such. We did not verify a single user, sale, key or price on any site.
2. Anything not read on a page we opened today is tagged "(unsourced, verify)" and must not be copied into public copy, a store listing or a comparison page.
3. Sites that block automated access on 2026-11-12 (GG.deals and SteamDB answered 403 with a bot challenge) are described only from their own indexed URLs and titles, and every claim about them is tagged "(unsourced, verify)".
4. Competitor prices are third-party prices. Our prices are a different thing: see `plans/sir-game-deals.md` section 7.
5. No assets, text or code are copied from any competitor.

Canonical file for SIR Game Deals. The old `plans/sir-deals.md` (r51, 2026-11-12, the games half) is superseded by this file and must not be quoted as current.

## 2. What the market actually looks like

Read from the pages and the probes of 2026-11-12, not from an aggregator.

1. **Steam has no Egyptian pound price. This is the fact the whole product stands on.** Our probe of the public Steam store endpoint on 2026-11-12 for Elden Ring (appid 1245620) returned `$39.99 USD` for `cc=EG`, while the same call for `cc=SA` returned `249.00 SR`, for `cc=AE` returned `249.00 AED` and for `cc=US` returned `$59.99`. A second title, Stardew Valley (appid 413150), returned `$5.99 USD` for `cc=EG`. So Steam recognises the country parameter, prices Saudi Arabia and the UAE in their own currency, and leaves Egypt on a US dollar tier. An Egyptian player never sees a price in pounds on Steam, and the real cost of a game moves with the dollar rate from one day to the next. Every honest price tracker for Egypt has to convert and to show the rate and the date it used.
2. **Egypt already has three good local products and they are all narrow.** HalfLoot (PlayStation, EGP, wallet payment, a human on WhatsApp), Games2Egypt (a shop with the regional gift-card catalogue) and NileShopper (a comparison site with a freshness rule) prove that Egyptian players want the final price in pounds, a mobile wallet instead of a card, and a price that was really seen recently. None of them tracks a game history, the lowest-ever price, a wishlist or the free games.
3. **The global aggregator is one click away and it knows the Egypt country code.** IsThereAnyDeal showed "Country: EG Currency: USD" in its own footer and has an EG selector in the header, which means the market exists and the demand is visible. But the interface is English first, the currency defaults to dollars even for Egypt, the cards show no history, and the third party key shops it pushes hardest are the ones with the worst region and activation risk for an Egyptian account.
4. **The two products that own the history, GG.deals and SteamDB, cannot be read by automation today.** Both answered our client with a bot challenge on 2026-11-12. That is a fact about our access, not about their quality, but it tells us one thing for our own build: a price history is a thing we must own from our own observations, because the community databases are built by scraping and they are not a lawful feed for us to copy.
5. **Free games are a separate product today.** GamerPower does giveaways well (130.1K members, an expires soon list, an API, RSS, a savings number) but does not do paid discounts or price history, and no aggregator tracks the giveaway calendar. Nobody joins a deal tracker and a giveaway tracker in one place.
6. **Payment is the local wedge.** HalfLoot's page leads with "no card needed, pay with InstaPay or Telda or any mobile wallet", and TechBites Store shows the same pattern. Any Egyptian product that asks for a card loses the sale.
7. **Console pricing has no public API.** Sony and Microsoft expose no public price feed, and PlayStation Store pages are not a lawful feed either. So console prices in v1 come from partner or affiliate feeds and from what our own users report, with the source and the date shown on every number.

## 3. Our edge, stated as five promises

1. **The price in pounds, with the rate and the date behind it.** Every offer shows the price in the visitor's currency, the original currency, the rate used and the moment it was observed, because Steam prices an Egyptian player in dollars.
2. **One screen for the discount and the free game.** A deal and a giveaway sit in the same list with the same filters, and the giveaway shows the hour it ends and the store that is giving it away.
3. **History we own, with the lowest-ever flag.** The price curve comes from our own observations, the lowest price ever is computed by us, and the day a price is stale the product says so instead of pretending.
4. **Neutral about the store, honest about the risk.** Official stores, Egyptian shops and third party key shops are listed in the same table with a trust tier, the region the key unlocks and the activation type, so nobody buys a card that will not work on their account.
5. **Alerts where Egyptians already are, and a free core.** Telegram alerts, an Arabic Egyptian interface, EGP pricing, and the whole browsing, history and free game tracker free for every player. Pro is for power users, not for basic use.
